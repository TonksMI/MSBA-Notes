# Personal Story - SHARE Model Version

## Matthew Tonks - Data Consulting Success Story

---

### **SITUATION - Setting the Context**

Hi, my name is Matthew Tonks. Early in my career at a small consulting firm, I found myself in a challenging situation that would define my professional approach to problem-solving.

*(pause)*

I had just completed two months of training on Informatica data pipeline tools when I was assigned to my first client project. The engagement was implementing a framework to process multiple EDI file formats - a critical system for their business operations.

*(pause)*

The project team was minimal: a project manager, a QA worker with limited qualifications, and me. The previous developer had been removed for poor performance, setting high expectations for my delivery.

---

### **HIGHLIGHT - The Key Challenge**

Here's where the real challenge emerged: I discovered the resume being sent to the client described someone with years of high-level experience with these tools. But I had barely two months of training on technology I didn't even know existed before joining the company.

*(pause)*

The project constraints made everything worse. We were limited to 40-hour work weeks, but daily standups took an hour due to team dysfunction, weekly planning meetings ran even longer, and mandatory knowledge-sharing sessions consumed our time. On a "good" week, we had only 24 productive hours out of 40.

*(pause)*

When deadlines hit, we had a completely unusable demo just one week before presentation. The gap between expectations and reality was enormous.

---

### **ACTION - What I Did**

Instead of continuing to grind through 16-hour days, I made three strategic decisions that changed everything:

*(pause)*

**First,** I took complete ownership of all technical aspects. I couldn't rely on our QA person, so I had to ensure everything was bulletproof for deployment.

**Second,** I did something that probably saved our contract: I started communicating directly with the client's team. No more hiding behind project management layers. I talked directly to their developers, hosted knowledge transfer sessions, and gathered usable feedback.

*(pause)*

**Third,** I focused on understanding their business needs rather than just technical requirements. This shift in perspective was crucial.

---

### **RESULT - What Happened**

The breakthrough came when the client realized the major improvements our framework would provide and how completely we covered their file processing needs.

*(pause)*

The demo went better than we thought possible. We not only met their technical requirements but exceeded their expectations for business value. The client was impressed with both the solution and the direct communication approach.

*(pause)*

More importantly, I discovered something fundamental about my career path: success wouldn't be defined by technical skills alone, but by adaptability and the ability to learn under pressure.

---

### **EVALUATION/ENHANCEMENT - Lessons Learned & Future Impact**

This experience taught me that **the more I succeed with technology, the less I need to rely on it alone.** The real power comes from combining technical competence with business understanding and effective communication.

*(pause)*

This realization created the opportunity to ask better questions and have the courage to search for answers when I don't have them immediately. It shaped my approach to every subsequent project and eventually led me to pursue data science, where the combination of technical skills and business insight is essential.

*(pause)*

**The lasting impact:** I learned that pressure doesn't just make diamonds - it reveals what's truly important. In my case, it revealed that adaptability, direct communication, and business focus are just as valuable as technical expertise.

This foundation has guided my career decisions and continues to influence how I approach complex problems in data science today.

---
